# ADR 0054: Irreversible Share candidate for the traveller LIVE pilot

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: **paused as research by [ADR 0065](0065-public-live-v1-policy.md) (2026-09-25).** Research, documentation and disconnected code are retained; no further implementation effort unless a future decision reopens it. Previously: user-selected implementation direction, protocol review pending; public traveller output remains prohibited.

> **Status update (2026-09-25):** Superseded by the direction brief. Irreversible Share and the traveller LIVE pilot protocol are archived; signals are public on Spots from the moment they are posted, and the author can delete them. See [PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md).

ADR 0055 records a later user-supplied community-summary alternative for review. It does not supersede this candidate or close its person-level privacy findings; it would require explicit approval of a different product/privacy contract.

## Context and disposition

ADR 0038 rejects threshold-only publication as a person-level privacy claim. ADRs 0051 and 0052 were rejected after review of withdrawal and release-time oracles. ADR 0053 proposes one person-level differentially private release over a finite pilot, but its later fixed authority cutoff cannot safely be reconstructed from current state. A lock-based cutoff can make another person's admission depend on the target's lock wait or deadline. The isolated sampler and V21 claim are disconnected prerequisites, not a public protocol.

This ADR proposes **the successful, explicit Share commit as the irreversible publication input**. The user selected this direction on 2026-09-23 after the four review blockers were explained. That selection authorizes implementation work, not a release approval or an irreversible user action in the current app. The existing immediate-suppression requirements in `LOCATION_PRIVACY.md` and related LIVE documentation need to distinguish private evidence from a future frozen public input. Exact disclosure, retention and the final protocol still require product/privacy and specialist review before activation. Private Quick Signals and their Stop semantics remain governed by the existing contract; no public reader, publisher, flag, or client flow may claim this ADR is active.

## Protected fact, pilot and output universe

The privacy unit is one independently verified natural person, across all accounts, journeys, devices and retries. Neighboring datasets add or remove that person's **entire pilot public Share input**; the input must contain at most one fixed histogram key. This is a statement about the public release mechanism, conditional on the same public pilot configuration and reader authorization context. It does not hide a person's own Share acknowledgement from that person, a compromised account, operator access, or external observations of their journey. Replacing one key with another is two add/remove changes and needs a separately stated composed bound. Human verification limits duplicate identities but does not prove travel, honest reporting or lack of collusion.

The proposed universe is frozen and published before the pilot opens: one identified 30-day Chennai/OMR pilot, at most ten reviewed coarse anchors, traffic only, four existing structured traffic values, and server-defined nonoverlapping five-minute windows. Thus `K <= 10 × 4 × 30 × 24 × 12 = 345,600` histogram keys. Each key is the exact tuple `(pilot version, anchor, traffic value, window)`; labels, geometry, endpoint exclusions, category meanings, pilot dates, publication and expiry times, and output schema are versioned and immutable for the entire pilot. No client-selected coordinate, anchor, route slice, category, event time, or new catalog version enlarges this universe. A changed catalog requires a separately reviewed pilot and privacy budget; it cannot silently reset a person's slot. A person can be assigned only one key for this pilot, not one per window or anchor.

The public release consists solely of a canonical, fixed-schedule projection: for each window and approved anchor, either no traveller condition or at most one coarse condition with a coarse, predetermined window label and expiry. Never expose raw reports, member lists, counts, confidence, exact event times, stable contributor IDs, receipt IDs or person-specific provenance. A canonical moment reference exists only for a released row and remains immutable. The result is an uncertain, privacy-protected estimate from volunteered reports, not verified road safety, a travel-time guarantee or evidence of physical presence. Provider alerts are separately sourced and labeled.

## Share commit is the input boundary

The owner deliberately shares **one selected accepted private Quick Signal** for this specific public purpose after seeing the irreversible disclosure. Passive private consent, receipt existence, route match, Google login, or a previously issued grant is insufficient. At one successful database commit, the server must (1) authenticate and authorize the owner and exact signal, (2) read current human-verification, account, journey, consent, context/catalog, safety and moderation authority under the established domain lock order, (3) derive exactly one canonical pilot key using server time and the frozen catalog, (4) claim the unique `(pilot_id, person_ref)` slot, and (5) durably freeze the minimal input and exact retry identity. All five either commit together or have no effect. The intended admission boundary is the fixed window close; client time or a later worker read cannot establish it. Unknown or stale authority, an unassessed or suspended person, an expired context or a failed transaction denies the new Share.

The first committed Share consumes the person's pilot slot permanently for the pilot, even if the private signal is subsequently stopped, the person changes accounts, the journey ends, or the account is deleted. An exact authenticated retry of that same committed command returns only the same private acknowledgement; it never claims a new key or increments a count. A conflicting retry, replacement signal, second account, new journey or later window cannot alter the frozen input. A lost response is uncertain until private owner recovery establishes the committed outcome; clients must not automatically submit a different Share on reconnect. The claim must survive deletion and re-verification through the documented pilot retention period without retaining identity documents or precise route data in the histogram path. That limited retention and deletion exception needs legal/product review and an enforceable cleanup schedule.

**Ordering rule:** an acknowledged Stop, Ghost Mode, consent withdrawal, completion, deletion, verification revocation or moderator restriction committed *before* Share must prevent that Share under the same serialized authority boundary. Once Share commits, none of those actions can retract, edit, rekey or refund its public histogram input. They stop new private/public contributions and remove discoverable individual presence and sensitive cached state immediately according to their own contracts. They do not remove a released aggregate or alter its metadata. An in-flight action's order is its database commit order, with no retroactive interpretation from a later read. Any current UI wording promising that Stop, Ghost Mode or deletion removes already Shared input would be false under this candidate and must be replaced before users can Share.

This irreversibility is the essential change from ADR 0053's later cutoff. It still requires proof that a target's Share, Stop or deletion cannot change **another person's** admission, timeout, cutoff result or public output through common locks, queueing, budget contention, cleanup or worker deadlines. The existing V21 claim alone does not provide that proof.

**Unresolved input seal:** checking database time before commit does not decide whether a transaction that checked just before window close but committed after it belongs in the public input. A later current-state read cannot reconstruct the exact accepted set. A seal that waits for in-flight writes can also let one person's transaction delay another person's admission or the release deadline. Before this ADR can be approved, specify and implement one linearization rule for Share admission, commit, window sealing and terminal decision that is stable across replicas and backup recovery. Prove or conservatively bound that adding/removing one person's Share cannot change any other person's inclusion or turn a fixed scheduled result into a missed-deadline response. A per-window `NO_RELEASE` fallback does not cure a data-dependent miss. If this cannot be established under the actual database and operational limits, keep traveller publication closed.

The independent feasibility review found a concrete counterexample: if enough other people have already contributed to make a condition likely, one person's transaction holding a shared window barrier across the fixed publication deadline forces either a late appearance or `NO_RELEASE` for everyone. The event that a condition appears on time can then differ with probability near one between neighboring executions; count noise does not protect it. A five-second transaction timeout, a pre-commit clock check, `NOWAIT`, `SKIP LOCKED` or serializable retry does not prove an exact commit-before-close rule or source-independent deadline. PostgreSQL can persist a logical `pg_snapshot` cut and test row visibility against it, but the actual snapshot time can move with workload and would not establish the promised wall-clock public transcript. This is a blocking protocol finding, not merely an unimplemented optimization. See the review ledger.

### Compatibility and migration gate

The current V18 `PublicSignalIntentService`/`JdbcPublicSignalIntentStore` records `ACTIVE` intent and changes it to `STOPPED`; the current UI describes Stop as stopping public consideration. Account deletion cascades those intent rows. That mutable intent **cannot** be reinterpreted as an irreversible public input, and an old `ACTIVE` row must never be migrated into a frozen Share or counted by a publisher. Introduce a separately versioned public Share command, durable frozen input and retained person claim, with explicit server response and client recovery language that distinguish (a) private signal Stop, (b) future Share admission, and (c) an already committed public input. Existing clients must fail closed at a version boundary; no legacy Stop response may promise retraction of a frozen input. Migration and rollback must preserve consumed claims and frozen decisions, never quietly reset a pilot budget or republish old intent. Test this with pre-migration `ACTIVE`/`STOPPED` rows, deleted accounts, lost responses, stale clients and exact retry identities.

V17's uniqueness constraint covers one **active account** per `person_ref`; verification rows cascade on deletion. That is not yet evidence that a deleted person receives the same reference on re-verification. An independently operated stable person-reference authority across deletion/re-entry, without identity documents in the signal path, is a hard prerequisite for the one-person pilot bound. If that authority cannot be established and audited under an approved retention policy, no person-level claim is available. The disconnected V21 claim must be bound atomically to the new frozen input; its existence cannot make mutable V18 intent safe to publish.

## Fixed randomized release and whole transcript

Retain ADR 0053's candidate sparse geometric mechanism. For **every fixed-universe key**, including absent keys, generate independent capped geometric `G1` and `G2` with the reviewed 64-bit sampler and let `Z = G1 - G2`. An observed key is eligible only when its count plus `Z` is at least 22. No zero-support key is emitted. For a fixed anchor/window, emit its coarse condition **only when exactly one traffic value survives**; zero or multiple surviving values produce no condition. This selector uses only the surviving-key set and frozen manifest, never raw counts or contributor data. Noise is generated once, with secure independent randomness, and the durable terminal decision records the frozen bytes or no-output result. Retries, replicas, failover and reads never redraw. Key discovery, database work, exception paths and release timing must not expose unnoised existence.

Choose and disclose fixed window close, first-visibility and expiry boundaries before opening the pilot. A proposed schedule is to close at five minutes, commit a terminal decision before ten minutes, first serve at ten minutes, and expire at fifteen minutes from window start; this schedule is **not approved** and must be checked against real processing time and utility. No row appears late or changes after first visibility. A missed decision may yield a terminal generic no-output for that window **only if** the miss and the fallback response are shown independent of every protected input; recovery cannot publish later. Whether the decision met its deadline, plus the resulting absence, status and timing, is part of the adversary's transcript. The decision and all related output metadata must be independent of subsequent source actions. If an operational outage or safety intervention can suppress or delay output, its trigger, scope and observable response must be independent of any person's input; otherwise it is part of the randomized privacy mechanism and requires a new proof. Per-key/data-dependent exception handling or target-driven emergency takedown is not covered by the candidate bound.

The adversary's observation transcript includes the union of all windows/anchors/values and all authorized readers' responses: row existence and absence, condition, reference, anchor/category/area and window labels, HTTP status and body shape, response size and timing, fixed publication and expiry timing, errors and outages, report targets and outcomes, cache and offline behavior, and changes after Stop, Ghost Mode, deletion, moderation and block actions. It also includes repeated reads, multiple accounts, replicas, devices and previously delivered copies. A public report or moderation response may reveal the canonical row but must not reveal which source was reported or change the row by source. Stale clients can retain already delivered bytes until fixed expiry; local Ghost, account change and journey end clear their own display immediately. They cannot erase copies held by others.

All authorized readers of a given canonical key receive the same frozen bytes or absence. A reader must have current authenticated ownership of an active journey and server-derived relevance to a frozen curated anchor; no arbitrary anchor query or coordinate scan is allowed. Reader denial, block policy, region-wide kill switches and rate limits need separately reviewed fixed rules that do not inspect the protected contribution or conditionally subtract evidence. The public DP claim covers the canonical release plus its delivery transcript only if these rules are independent of the protected person's input. Source-dependent denial, per-viewer filtering, mutable freshness, streaming updates, notifications, caches and exports are outside this candidate and must remain closed until reviewed. No source-driven invalidation may resurrect or suppress a released window.

For add/remove neighboring pilot datasets **if** the one-person/one-key bound and full observable-transcript conditions hold, ADR 0053 proposes `(epsilon = ln 2, delta <= 1/3,145,728 + 3K·2^-63)` for the entire 30-day pilot; at the maximum `K`, the cap term is about `1.13 × 10^-13`. This follows the candidate proof for existing-key count changes and the exceptional probability that a newly observed singleton crosses threshold 22, followed by fixed post-processing. It is **not** an independently verified or operational privacy guarantee. Every additional pilot, revised key universe, resampling, source-dependent failure or transcript channel requires composition or a new analysis. The bound allows some inference, including the stated `delta` exceptional event; it does not promise anonymity against external observation or truthful traffic conditions.

## Failure, retention and operational boundaries

The Share transaction, one-person claim, input freeze and exact retry identity must be durable and correct across replicas and account deletion. Publication uses a single durable terminal decision per fixed window/anchor, revision fencing and a consistent lock order. Missing authority, mismatched version, unavailable storage, randomness failure, stale replica, or inability to finalize before the fixed boundary denies new Share or the **entire affected scheduled release** under a reviewed source-independent rule. No replica may serve a provisional decision, re-evaluate eligibility from mutable current state, publish a late result or expose a source-specific error. A database backup/restore or rollback must not restore a consumed slot or redraw a released key. A failover drill must cover already delivered clients and the exact frozen transcript.

No public cache or realtime fan-out is authorized by this ADR. If introduced later, its keys, invalidation, TTL, replay and cross-replica delivery must preserve the same bytes and schedule. Logs, traces, metrics, reports and moderation records must omit precise location and any public link from a moment to a contributor; restricted internal audit and claim records require explicit retention, access and deletion policy. New serving ends at fixed expiry, but an observer may keep captured output indefinitely and combine it with later pilots; the one-person budget record persists for the pilot plus justified retention. Operators must be able to disable the **whole** traveller pilot on a source-independent emergency rule without selectively changing one row; the impact of such a shutdown on the transcript requires independent review before use.

## Alternatives and consequences

- **Later fixed authority cutoff / lock-based freeze:** rejected as a candidate here because current state cannot reconstruct an earlier snapshot and lock waits or deadline misses can make another person's output depend on a target action.
- **Contributor-dependent suppression after Share:** rejected by ADR 0051's disappearance oracle.
- **Immutable deterministic threshold:** rejected by ADR 0052 as a person-level claim and unbounded across repeated releases.
- **No traveller-derived public output:** remains the safe default and must be selected if disclosure, utility or proof obligations fail. Provider-sourced utility can continue under its separate gate.

Irreversible Share makes Stop/Ghost/deletion semantics harder to explain and requires narrowly retained anti-duplication data. Threshold 22 likely yields little output in sparse windows; utility must be measured with consented real pilot-density distributions, false-reassurance and wrong-value rates, and latency. No fabricated condition or lowered privacy threshold may be used to rescue utility without a new decision.

## Required evidence before any approval

1. Product/privacy and legal review approve the exact plain-language Share, Stop, Ghost Mode and deletion disclosures, retention exception, and changes to conflicting LIVE/privacy specs. Users must know before Share that future withdrawal cannot remove the frozen input or an already delivered aggregate. Versioned API/client acknowledgements and recovery controls must use this language; existing V18 Stop copy must not be silently reused.
2. Independent mathematical review covers the 64-bit sampler, secure randomness, fixed-key sparse threshold, absent-key behavior, post-processing, cap error, neighboring relation, key replacement, pilot-to-pilot composition and the **entire observable transcript**, including source-independent operational failures and reader authorization.
3. A concurrency design and PostgreSQL tests prove Share atomicity with the verified-person claim, exact idempotency, account/journey/consent/context/safety authority, Stop/Ghost/completion/deletion and moderation; prove a person's intervention cannot cause another person's missed admission or release deadline. Test conflicting accounts, replicas, retries, rollback, restart and backup restore.
4. A durable publisher and reader pass adversarial tests for sparse/absent keys, all fixed keys, secure one-time draws, no-output finalization, late worker, version drift, partial failure, replica disagreement, status/body/timing differences, block toggles, reports, caches and offline copies. Authenticated object-level authorization and no caller-selected anchor/coordinate must be exercised end to end.
5. Human verification and operator review, report intake/moderation, block-safe delivery, anti-abuse budgets, regional catalog and endpoint exclusions, real OAuth/provider configuration, retention/cleanup monitoring, load testing and rendered device QA meet the separate LIVE release ledger. Tests of isolated V21 and sampler primitives do not satisfy these gates.
6. Real, consented Chennai/OMR density data show useful output at threshold 22 without misleading safety claims. An independent adversarial privacy/security review examines the final implementation and records approval in a later release decision. This ADR itself never turns on publication.

## Current blockers

V22 now provides a disconnected internal Share-to-claim/input foundation and owner recovery for code review; it has no endpoint, flag, UI or publisher. This foundation does not settle the fixed-time window seal or cross-person deadline dependence. The current private Share/Stop path and product copy have not been reconciled with irreversibility. Source-dependent operational failure has no proof. The actual delivery/report/cache transcript, stable one-person verification operations, deletion retention policy, regional utility and device behavior remain unvalidated. Therefore this candidate cannot be approved or exposed now.
