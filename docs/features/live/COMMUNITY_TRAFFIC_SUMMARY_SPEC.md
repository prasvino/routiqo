# Community-reported traffic summary — V3 staging feature contract

Status: **V3 implementation and staging evaluation authorized on 2026-09-23; production activation not approved**. This contract originated in the user-supplied handoff and [ADR 0055](../../adr/0055-consented-community-traffic-summary-proposal.md). The existing ADR 0053/0054 person-level DP research code and documentation remain preserved but disconnected; further research is paused. Public traveller output stays disabled in production.

## Purpose and scope

Offer an optional, recent, coarse traffic summary inside an authenticated active-journey LIVE list. Traffic only, using existing structured values and reviewed server-selected coarse road anchors. Do not publish exact position, endpoints, individual markers, names, receipt IDs, timestamps, report counts, supporter percentages, text, media or a searchable public map. Distinguish community reports from official provider alerts. A summary is uncertain and may be wrong; “No recent community update” never means a road is clear.

The proposed contract is **consented aggregation with residual participation-inference risk**. A threshold, removed name, account quota or committed database snapshot is not differential privacy or legal anonymity. A reader with colluding accounts, outside observations or repeated-window knowledge may infer participation. The owner authorized implementation with knowledge of this difference but has not accepted the different privacy terms for production activation.

## Deliberate Share and recovery

The private Quick Signal stays private unless its owner explicitly selects it and confirms a new versioned public purpose. Check current authenticated account, owned active journey, receipt/grant, private consent generation, context/catalog, verification and safety restriction. The server selects the approved anchor and nonoverlapping five-minute observation window. No client coordinate, anchor or event-time authority. Existing V18 `ACTIVE` intent, V21 claim and V22 frozen Share are neither consent nor candidates for V3; never auto-migrate them.

One successful new candidate per eligible **account per window across all anchors and traffic values**. A proposed staging daily limit is 12 successful new submissions per account per UTC day pending abuse-policy review. Enforce both with database constraints/debits; conflicting retries cannot add votes or consume another debit. Exact owner-bound retries and fresh-device recovery return the committed candidate outcome. A network failure is uncertain; never send a different signal automatically. An accepted candidate has no guarantee of inclusion or publication, since a delayed commit may miss the snapshot. Do not shift it to a later window.

Draft owner disclosure for product review:

> Share this traffic report to help create a recent community traffic summary for this road area. Other eligible travellers will not see your name or exact location in the summary. Someone with other information may still infer that you contributed. You can stop future sharing, but a summary already being prepared or published may still include this report. Published summaries expire from Routiqo; copies saved by others cannot be recalled.

Use an unchecked, per-report deliberate action; do not submit offline, automatically on reconnect, while navigating, or after stale account/journey/context/consent authority. Keep stopped/passenger safety confirmation separate. Owner states: idle, submitting, accepted for consideration, exact retry recovered, uncertain, conflict, stopped for future sharing, offline and unavailable. Never say “your report went live” on candidate acceptance.

## Snapshot, Stop and terminal result

The publication transaction's actual PostgreSQL MVCC snapshot decides the eligible candidate set. A Stop, Ghost Mode, consent withdrawal, completion, deletion or restriction committed before it excludes the candidate; one committed after it may not affect that summary. The owner response must be truthful about this boundary and never promise a guaranteed cancellation of work already in progress. A failed publication has no public result and a bounded retry uses a new snapshot. Once a terminal decision commits, retries do not re-evaluate or move candidates. An uncommitted Share held across the snapshot is omitted and must not block the publisher.

One canonical terminal decision per schema/catalog configuration version, anchor and window is `NO_OUTPUT` or a coarse condition. Persist the decision and projection atomically; a later authorized safety suppression changes serving state through a separate audited record without redrawing or rewriting the decision. The staging rule is 12 distinct eligible accounts total, a unique value with at least 10 supporters and at least 80% support; no synthetic fallback and no claim that this is a privacy threshold. A newly committed candidate after the terminal snapshot cannot reopen it. A summary is served only while unexpired and labels its observation period, not “live now.” Staging aims to process shortly after close, ideally within one minute, and forbids serving after window end plus ten minutes; these are settings to validate, not guaranteed fixed first visibility.

## Reader, report and moderation boundary

Readers need authenticated ownership of an active journey, current server-derived context/relevance and shared rate control. No caller-selected anchor, coordinate, arbitrary area or enumeration. All authorized readers receive the same canonical row, with an opaque random reference, approved coarse label, structured value, observation-period label, expiry, source and schema version only. The response envelope also carries server time solely to let clients conservatively expire delivered rows despite device-clock skew. No per-viewer source subtraction. Block semantics for unattributed aggregates need explicit review; the UI must not claim that blocked users' reports were filtered.

A report targets only a canonical moment visible to that owner, with bounded reason/idempotency and abuse limit. One report cannot automatically remove output. Moderator suppression is audited and can end serving; it does not hide the fact that another observer may already have a copy. Start with foreground HTTP refresh, no realtime fan-out, public CDN cache or persistent offline community feed. Clear local rows on logout/account change, Ghost Mode and journey completion; expire by absolute server time and reauthorize on reconnect. Distinguish loading, no recent update, offline/stale, unavailable, expired and suppressed states. Verify small screens, large text, keyboard/screen reader and reduced motion on rendered devices.

## Storage, operations and retention proposal

Use separate versioned candidate, exact retry/debit, terminal-decision and canonical projection storage; do not cascade-delete public projections with source accounts. PostgreSQL `REPEATABLE READ` is a proposed selection mechanism, with publisher ownership obtained before the input snapshot and a unique terminal key. Because the first non-transaction-control statement fixes that snapshot, the ownership-acquisition statement itself must be tested for stale-snapshot behavior. Multi-replica race tests must verify snapshot semantics, ownership, rollback, late commits, Stop/Ghost/completion/deletion/restriction order, restore/failover and no provisional output. Isolation alone does not enforce business uniqueness. Bound query size, transaction duration, worker concurrency, retries and cleanup; distinguish genuine empty output from unavailable storage without revealing raw counts.

Implemented staging settings: source candidates logically expire 24 hours after window end; public serving ends ten minutes after window end; expired projection content is removed within another 24 hours. Daily debit rows are eligible for cleanup after two UTC days; report and suppression-audit rows after 30 days. Minimal terminal tombstones remain to prevent reopening. Account-bound retry disappears with its source candidate, while backups need a reviewed schedule. Public read expiry applies even when cleanup lags. No identity documents, precise routes or GPS trails enter publication storage. These staging intervals and physical/backup deletion behavior require product/privacy and operational approval before production use. Maintenance has its own disabled-by-default flag so it can continue after serving rollback.

## Acceptance and activation gates

1. Implementation is authorized; a separate explicit production decision must accept changing from the selected person-level DP goal to this aggregation contract, with residual inference, after-snapshot withdrawal and possible safety takedown plainly disclosed. Product/privacy and security review approve consent, blocks, retention and moderation policy.
2. Versioned authenticated transport, owner recovery/Stop, PostgreSQL publisher, canonical reader/report/moderation, cleanup, generated contracts, client states and runbook are implemented behind a disabled production flag. No V18 or V22 migration supplies consent.
3. Real PostgreSQL tests cover duplicate/conflicting submissions, debit/retry, before/after-snapshot withdrawal, in-flight Share, two publishers, abort/retry, expiry and restore. HTTP tests cover owner/relevance, guessed references, rate limits and account isolation. Rendered browser/device tests cover Share, Stop, reload, offline and accessibility.
4. Staging measures low/medium/high density, repeat commuters, daily cap, conflicting/malicious accounts, output coverage and age, and accuracy against appropriately collected ground truth. Synthetic scenarios are labeled; no threshold reduction rescues sparse utility. Real OAuth, reviewed regional catalog/provider configuration and retention operations pass their separate release gates.

Until these gates close, existing private Quick Signals and official provider alerts keep their separate behavior; no traveller-derived public output is enabled.
